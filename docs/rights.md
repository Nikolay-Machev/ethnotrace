# Source rights and attribution

This import retains the source-specific rights statements and citations from the dataset snapshot. It does not apply a blanket open licence to source publications, community knowledge or database compilations.

The scientific full-text extractions cite article-specific Creative Commons statements where identified. The two settler publications and community webpages have copyright or unresolved open-reuse status. The Duke archive row is marked CC0 at the archive level; this does not erase its underlying ethnobotanical provenance. GBIF records retain the existing note that the underlying backbone licence was not independently verified. No claims about training permissions are inferred from public availability.

The repository includes factual metadata, curated summaries, citations and short source fragments already in the source snapshot. It does not include full article texts, full abstracts, original PDFs, photos, community webpage copies or raw interview data. Supporting fragments may establish only part of a record and must be interpreted with their source locator.

Community attribution and access notes remain in each raw/normalized record. Researcher reports of traditional knowledge are distinguished from community-published accounts. Do not assign a precise Nation, plant part or preparation when the original source does not establish it. Permission to access an online document is not permission to collect new restricted knowledge.

The original reference documents are in `imports/dataset/docs/references.md` and `imports/dataset/docs/papers.md`. The processed reference index preserves citations and rights notes; preprocessing does not independently verify the truth or completeness of those notes. Before broader redistribution or model training, review permissions for the particular source and intended use.
