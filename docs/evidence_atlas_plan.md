# Next scope: evidence-gap atlas

Research question: which documented Canadian traditional plant uses have been experimentally investigated, and how closely do those experiments match the recorded use?

The proposed atlas extends the current evidence explorer. It is planned work, not an implemented feature.

1. Show all documented accounts as rows and comparison dimensions as columns: species, part, preparation, outcome and experimental setting. Distinguish documented matches, differences and missing information. Keep experimental direction separate.
2. Allow each cell to open the cited account–experiment comparison. Count unique publications separately from experimental arms and repeated traditional accounts.
3. Add filters for community attribution, region, plant and endpoint only where those fields are documented. Avoid suggesting national representativeness or ranking communities by evidence availability.
4. Present research gaps: missing preparation documentation, untested recorded symptoms, evidence limited to cells or animals, and conflicting endpoint findings. Missing indexed evidence is not proof that no research exists.
5. Optionally add semantic retrieval as a challenger to the transparent baseline, using fixed source-grouped evaluation and source passages. Do not equate semantically similar symptoms with the same medical claim.

Hackathon scope: a cited matrix, one drill-down workflow, three clear example comparisons and a reproducible ranking comparison. A full knowledge graph, automated discovery pipeline and fine-tuned classifier can follow only if time and evidence justify them.

The technical contribution is traceable comparison across heterogeneous sources, explicit uncertainty and measurable retrieval, not a claim to validate treatments.
